How to Play Minesweeper

Minesweeper is a logic puzzle played on a grid of hidden squares. Some squares hide mines. Your goal is to open every safe square without ever opening a mine.

When you open a square, it shows a number. The number tells you how many of the eight surrounding squares contain a mine. A square showing 1 has exactly one mine next to it, and a square showing 3 has three. If a square shows nothing, none of its neighbors are mines, so the game opens the whole empty area for you.

Controls

  • Touch: tap a square to dig it. Press and hold for about half a second to plant or remove a flag.
  • Mouse: left click digs a square, and right click plants or removes a flag.
  • Chording: tap or click an opened number whose surrounding flags already match it, and the game opens all of its remaining neighbors at once.
  • First move: your first dig is always safe, so you cannot lose on the very first tap.

Difficulty Levels

Easy uses a 9 x 9 board, Medium uses 11 x 11, and Hard uses 14 x 14. Larger boards have smaller squares and more mines, so you have to keep track of more information at the same time. Changing the level or pressing Restart always starts a fresh board.

Strategy Tips

  • Start from the numbers. After your first dig, look for a number whose count already equals the number of hidden squares around it. Every one of those squares is a mine.
  • Flag what you are sure about. Flags help you count. When a number has all of its mines flagged, every other neighbor is safe to open.
  • Learn the 1-2-1 pattern. Three numbers 1, 2, 1 in a row along an edge usually mean that the two outer hidden squares are mines and the middle one is safe.
  • Work along edges and corners. Squares at the border have fewer neighbors, so their numbers give you stronger clues.
  • Use the mine counter. The counter at the top shows how many mines are left. Near the end of a game it can settle a situation that the numbers alone cannot.
  • Guess only as a last resort. If no safe move exists, pick the square that is least likely to be a mine, and avoid squares next to many numbers.
